# Digital Publishing Opportunity Intelligence: Clustering Amazon Kindle Books

This project explores the Amazon Kindle Books Dataset 2023 (about 133,000 e-books in 31 categories) to find out whether Kindle books fall into natural market segments and whether some segments carry the bestseller badge more often. It is the first step of a larger idea: helping new authors and small publishers identify promising niches. The notebook follows a step-by-step workflow: loading and quick EDA, data quality checks, feature preparation and scaling, three clustering methods (K-Means, hierarchical clustering and DBSCAN), a comparison of the methods, a PCA visualisation, and a profile of each cluster, including a closer look at Children's eBooks.

Books are clustered on four reliable features: log-transformed price, star rating, Kindle Unlimited membership and title length. The `reviews` column is deliberately not used, because it is zero for every book in 11 categories and for more than half of the books in 15 categories, which points to a data collection problem. K-Means with k = 3 gives three profiles: mainstream publisher books (about 52%), cheap Kindle Unlimited books (about 27%) and long-title books (about 21%). The Kindle Unlimited group carries the bestseller badge about 2 to 6 times more often than the others, but this is an association, not proof of cause. The structure is moderate, and the three methods give a broadly similar picture.

The data has important limits: it contains no sales figures and no time series, the success flags are a single snapshot that favours well-known authors, about 37% of publication dates are missing, and the number of books per category appears to be capped, so category size is not market size. Planned next steps are joining the data with Amazon Reviews 2023 through ASIN to measure demand over time, adding Google Trends for seasonality, testing methods for mixed data (K-Prototypes), and returning to the supervised idea of predicting the bestseller badge. To run the notebook, place `kindle_data-v2.csv` in the working folder (or change the path in the load cell) and use "Run all"; it needs `pandas`, `numpy`, `matplotlib`, `seaborn`, `scikit-learn` and `scipy`.
